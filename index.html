CASE STUDY • OPEN INNOVATION

🌍 Connect + Develop:
How P&G Opened Its Innovation Network

Explore how Procter & Gamble moved beyond a closed innovation model and connected its capabilities with ideas from the wider world.

👥 How to use this activity: Read the case study, discuss the questions with your group, and record your ideas in the response boxes. Use evidence from the case to support your reasoning.

1️⃣ Background

🧴 Procter & Gamble (P&G) is one of the world's largest consumer goods companies. You probably know many of its brands, including Tide, Pampers, Gillette, Oral-B and Olay.

For decades, P&G relied on a closed innovation model. The company employed thousands of scientists and invested billions of dollars in research and development (R&D). Its philosophy was simple:

“The smartest people work for us, so innovation should happen inside the company.”

For many years, this approach worked well.

2️⃣ The Problem 🚨

By 2000, P&G's innovation system was struggling.

📉Innovation success rates had stagnated
📈Research costs continued to increase
🐢Product development was becoming slower
⚔️Competition was becoming more intense

The company realised that continuing to rely solely on internal R&D would not deliver the growth it needed.

At the same time, universities, suppliers, entrepreneurs, start-ups and research laboratories were generating valuable innovations outside large organisations.

🌍 A powerful insight: P&G estimated that for every scientist working inside the company, there were around 200 equally capable scientists and engineers working elsewhere in the world.
Why try to invent everything ourselves when great ideas already exist outside the company?

3️⃣ The Solution 💡

In 2000, CEO 👨‍💼 A.G. Lafley launched a new innovation strategy called:

🔗 Connect + Develop

Instead of inventing everything internally, P&G would actively search for ideas, technologies and solutions from outside organisations.

The company began collaborating with:

🏫Universities
🏭Suppliers
🚀Start-ups
👩‍🔬Independent inventors
🤝Research organisations and competitors
🎯 Lafley set a bold target: 50% of P&G's innovations should come from outside the company.

4️⃣ How It Worked 🔄

Before Connect + Develop, P&G operated like a fortress 🏰.

  • Problems stayed inside the company.
  • Solutions had to be invented internally.
  • Innovation was expensive and time-consuming.

After Connect + Develop, P&G became an innovation network 🌐. The company identified problems, shared them with external partners and searched globally for proven solutions.

❓ “How can we invent this?”

became:

❓ “Who may have already solved this problem?”

The company then combined external ideas with its own strengths in manufacturing, marketing, branding and distribution.

1. IdentifyDefine the problem
2. ConnectFind external expertise
3. AdaptCombine ideas and capabilities
4. LaunchCommercialise the solution

5️⃣ Real Examples 🎯

🧹

Swiffer Duster

P&G wanted a better dust-cleaning product. It partnered with a Japanese organisation that had already developed the required material technology.

Benefits: Faster innovation, lower development cost and successful product launch.

✨

Olay Regenerist

The key anti-ageing ingredient did not come from P&G's laboratories. A supplier had already developed a peptide technology. P&G licensed the innovation and transformed it into a highly successful global product.

🥔

Pringles Prints

P&G wanted to print images and messages directly onto Pringles crisps. It found a university professor in Italy who had developed a similar printing system for cakes and biscuits. P&G adapted the technology and launched the product in less than one year.

Benefits: Lower cost, lower risk and faster time to market.

6️⃣ The Results 📊

  • ✅ The proportion of innovations containing externally sourced elements increased from approximately 15% to more than 35%.
  • ✅ Around 45% of new development projects included externally sourced technologies.
  • ✅ R&D productivity increased by nearly 60%.
  • ✅ Innovation success rates more than doubled.
  • ✅ Innovation costs fell.
Competitive advantage no longer came only from inventing ideas. It also came from finding, adapting and commercialising ideas more effectively than competitors.
